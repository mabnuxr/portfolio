Case Study

Scalekit

Founding Designer

Agent auth infrastructure, from the ground up.

scalekit.com ↗

Context

Scalekit is a developer platform providing auth infrastructure for teams building AI agents and MCP integrations — SSO, SCIM provisioning, and OAuth 2.0 token lifecycle management, aimed at making agent identity and access as rigorous as human identity and access.

I joined as the founding designer, drawn to the 0-to-1 scope of shaping a product's entire design language and system from scratch rather than joining an established team. There was no existing design system and no prior product to inherit — the product, the component library, and the design practice were all built in parallel.

The Problem

AI agents were becoming first-class actors inside enterprise software — reading data, calling APIs, taking actions on a human's behalf — but most auth systems still modeled identity as strictly human. The design challenge was to make agent authentication feel as legible and trustworthy as human authentication: SSO and provisioning flows that developers could reason about, and a token lifecycle model that made it clear who — or what — was acting, and with what scope of access, at every step.

What I Owned

  • SAML/OIDC-based SSO flows
  • SCIM provisioning flows
  • OAuth 2.0 token lifecycle interfaces
  • MCP gateway tooling
  • Agent Auth quickstart, with AI-assisted setup paths for Claude Code, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot
  • The Figma component library used across every product surface

Approach

Operating as a PM/design hybrid, I split time between strategy, research, and hands-on interaction and system design, working directly with engineering and GTM. Because the product and the design system were being built simultaneously, most of the work was defining primitives first — components, patterns, and terminology — then applying them consistently as new surfaces came online: SSO setup, token management, the agent auth quickstart.

Why It Matters

This is the clearest expression of my broader thesis: as software increasingly acts on our behalf, the permission and trust boundary between a human and the AI agents operating for them has to be designed deliberately, not bolted on. Scalekit's auth infrastructure is literally that boundary — who an agent is, what it's allowed to do, and how a human stays in control of that delegation.
