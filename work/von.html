Case Study

VON

Contract Product Designer

Access control for an AI-native RevOps platform.

vonlabs.ai ↗

Context

VON lets revenue teams query CRM data, calls, and pipeline in natural language — an AI-native platform built on Claude Code and MCP-native throughout. VON's platform was built on top of Scalekit's auth infrastructure, which I had designed, and that connection is part of why I was brought in: to solve the platform's access-control gap.

Unlike Scalekit, this wasn't a 0-to-1 project. VON already had a live product and an existing (if minimal) permissions system I had to design around and extend, not replace wholesale.

The Problem

VON shipped with only two roles — admin and member — and no granular permissions, team-level access, or sharing model. As the customer base grew, that flat model stopped holding: enterprise buyers expected role-based access, license tiers, and control over who could see or act on what. The challenge was designing a full access model on top of a live product, without a rewrite and without disrupting customers already using it.

What I Owned

  • Roles and license tiers
  • Object-level and inherited permissions
  • Team-level access
  • Integration-level scoping
  • A unified sharing experience used across the app

Approach

I benchmarked against Notion, Linear, Salesforce, Figma, and Slack to understand how mature products handle the same tradeoffs — hierarchy, inheritance, and where team-level access ends and object-level access begins — then designed within VON's existing live Storybook/Tailwind/shadcn system rather than in a Figma-only flow disconnected from the product. I worked directly with the founder/CEO and engineering leadership at a high production-fidelity bar, covering empty and error states, not just the happy path.

Why It Matters

VON is an AI-native product where agents and humans both act on customer data — which makes the access model the same trust boundary I care about generally, just from the human-permissions side rather than the agent-identity side I worked on at Scalekit. Getting that boundary right — who can see what, and how access is scoped and shared — is what let VON credibly sell into enterprise accounts.
